# Reference photographs for the photoreal London

Twelve real photographs of London, looked at next to the screenshots in docs/london/shots/ and never
shipped: the files live in docs/london/reference/, which is gitignored. Run the download again from the
source pages below (1920 px wide copies through Special:FilePath; 05 at 960 px, the size Commons would
serve when this was written).

The site's London views (src/city/cities.js). The landscape view looks east-north-east (bearing 68
degrees) from 9.3 km off and 1.4 km up, 8.5 degrees down, from over Putney and Barnes: Westminster and the
London Eye in front, the South Bank, then the City and its towers. The portrait view looks west (bearing
276) from 10.5 km off and 2.6 km up, 14 degrees down, from over Charlton, just east of Greenwich: the City
and the Shard in front, Westminster behind. No photographs on Commons are taken from the landscape view's place in the air; the
nearest are from airliners on the Heathrow approach, which fly over central London westbound at 1 to 2
km, and from the hills of Greenwich and Blackheath on the portrait view's bearing.

All are photographs on Wikimedia Commons with a camera named in their metadata; none is a render, a
composite or AI-generated as far as their pages show. Chosen by a rule fixed before looking at the
renders: Commons' "Aerial photographs of the City of London", "... of the River Thames in London", "...
of London at night", "Views from Greenwich Park" and "Rain in London" / "Clouds in London", colour, not
HDR-tonemapped, then the one nearest the site's views for each condition. Geograph's aerial series could
not be fetched at full size (Commons throttled them); one is used at 960 px.

## What the photographs show (written before the work, to compare against)

1. Air. The far side of the city fades to a white-grey (03, 07), and on grey days the City's towers are
   pale shapes on the horizon (06).
2. Materials. A city of brick and stone: Victorian terraces of yellow-grey London stock brick and red
   brick under grey slate (08), white Portland stone for the public buildings and the City's older offices
   (02), honey-coloured limestone at Westminster; the City's towers in glass, blue-green and grey, with
   bright reflections of the sky (01). Roofs a darker grey than the walls, flat roofs grey and black.
3. Water. The Thames is brown-grey, opaque with silt, wide (250 m at Westminster); it reflects the sky
   palely and shows the wakes of boats (01, 02, 04). At low tide mud and shingle show along its edges.
4. Thin things. The London Eye is a white rim and spokes, see-through (02, 04); the bridges are dark lines.
5. Light. Grey most of the time (04 to 07); when the sun is out, low and from the south, it lights the
   south-facing fronts of the terraces gold (08).
6. Night. The street grid in white LED and some orange (09, 10), the river a black ribbon, the City's
   offices white; under low cloud the cloud glows orange from below (11).
7. Rain. A dark deck with rain falling as grey curtains, the skyline still lit in a gap (12).
