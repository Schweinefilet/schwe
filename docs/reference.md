# Reference photographs for the photoreal New York

Twelve real photographs of Manhattan, all from the south or south-west over the harbour, the direction the
site's New York view looks from (bearing 35 degrees, north-north-east up the island, 14 degrees down for
a landscape window, 24 for a portrait one; src/city/cities.js). They are looked at next to the
screenshots in docs/shots/, never shipped: the files live in docs/reference/, which is gitignored. Run
the download again from the source pages below (1920 px wide copies were used).

All are photographs on Wikimedia Commons. None is a render, a composite or AI-generated as far as their
pages show: each page states a camera or a photographer's account. Chosen by the rule used for the night's
calibration (DECISIONS.md 2026-11-20): Commons search results for Manhattan from the air or across the
harbour, colour, not HDR, then the ones nearest the site's bearing for each condition.

The views do not match the site's exactly. Most are from lower (a helicopter at 300 to 1,000 m, an
airliner at 2 to 3 km, Staten Island's shore) and nearer, with wider lenses. The site's camera is 4.1 km
up and 17 km off. What carries over: the colour and contrast of the air, the materials, the water, the
light. What does not: the perspective (theirs converges more), and how much of the surroundings shows.

## What the photographs show (written before the work, to compare against)

1. Air. Every daytime photograph has a veil that grows with distance: Midtown is paler, bluer and lower
   in contrast than the Financial District (03 most, 02 at the horizon). Shadows are never black; they are
   blue-grey and lifted. In 03 the far end of the island is nearly the colour of the sky.
2. Materials. Not one colour. Dark glass towers (blue-black, green-black) beside pale stone and beige
   brick; red-brown brick tenements and brownstones in the low middle of the island; white and grey
   concrete. Roofs are a darker, greyer mosaic than walls, with mechanical clutter. The parks are green
   in summer, rust and olive in November (01).
3. Glass. Curtain walls facing the sky are lighter and bluer; facing the camera they are dark with sky
   reflected at the edges. Glass towers show their floor lines faintly.
4. Water. Deep blue to slate under a clear sky (01, 02), green-grey under haze (04), lead-grey in rain
   (10, 11). Wakes and wind texture are visible even from airliner height. The waterfront reflects the
   buildings only close in and in calm water.
5. Light. At noon in November the sun is low (30 degrees): long shadows north, the south faces bright.
   At golden hour the tops of the towers are orange while the streets are already in shadow (05). At
   blue hour the sky is the brightest thing and the windows start to show (06).
6. Night. The street grid is the brightest structure (07, 08): orange-gold lines, white on the avenues,
   with dark blocks between. Towers are speckled with lit windows, mostly warm white; Times Square and
   Hudson Yards are white-violet blazes (09). Water is black with long reflections of the waterfront.
   The sky glows dull orange-brown with the city's own light.
7. Rain. The air goes grey and the far end of the island disappears (10, 11). Contrast collapses, there
   are no shadows, the water is lead-grey and flat. Rain shafts are visible as streaks from a distance
   (11). In 12 the tops of the towers are in the cloud.
