# Reference photographs for the photoreal Paris

Twelve real photographs of Paris, looked at next to the screenshots in docs/paris/shots/ and never
shipped: the files live in docs/paris/reference/, which is gitignored. Run the download again from the
source pages below (1920 px wide copies, through Special:FilePath).

The site's Paris view (src/city/cities.js) looks east-north-east (bearing 78 degrees) from 9 km off and
1.3 km up, 7.5 degrees down, over Sèvres and Saint-Cloud, with the Eiffel Tower in front. Nine of the
twelve are from the Parc de Saint-Cloud (the Domaine national, about 100 m above the Seine, 6 to 7 km
from the tower) on the same bearing: the same composition from lower, with the tower 6 to 8 degrees
nearer the horizon than the site has it. Two are from the Eiffel Tower's top looking east and east-south-
east, over the frame's middle distance at the site's angle of depression (5 to 10 degrees). Two are
night aerials from airliners, steeper than the site.

All are photographs on Wikimedia Commons with a camera named in their metadata; none is a render, a
composite or AI-generated as far as their pages show. Chosen by a rule fixed before looking at the
renders: Commons' "Views of Paris from Saint-Cloud", "Views from the third floor of the Eiffel Tower"
and "Aerial photographs of Paris at night", colour, not HDR-tonemapped, then the one nearest the site's
bearing for each condition.

## What the photographs show (written before the work, to compare against)

1. Air. At 6 km the Eiffel Tower is already paler than the trees in front of it (01, 04); on a winter day
   (04) it is nearly the colour of the sky, and the east of the city is gone. Shadows are blue-grey, never
   black.
2. Materials. A field of cream: limestone and pale render, slightly warm, with the grey-blue band of
   zinc roofs on top of almost every building (03, 07). Chimney stacks in rows with orange pots. Post-war
   towers (the Front de Seine, Montparnasse) in grey concrete and dark glass stand out. Trees in the
   courtyards and along the avenues, and the big parks (the Champ de Mars) olive and rust in autumn.
3. The tower. Brown-bronze, see-through at its base and between its legs, the haze showing through the
   lattice; seen from 6 km it is a fine dark lace, never a solid (01, 04, 06).
4. Water. The Seine is olive-grey and narrow, its banks lined with stone quays and plane trees; under a
   deck lead-grey (05).
5. Light. In the afternoon the sun is behind Saint-Cloud: the facades facing the lens are lit, warm at
   golden hour (06, 07), with long shadows across the streets. Under a deck (04, 05, 12) there are no
   shadows; the city reads by its albedo and the dark slots of its streets.
6. Night. The tower gold, its beacon sweeping; the city a carpet of orange-gold points with the avenues
   brightest (09, 10); under cloud the sky glows orange-brown over the city (08). Windows are a faint warm
   speckle.
7. Rain. A grey veil over the city, the far side gone, the near side low in contrast (11, 12).
